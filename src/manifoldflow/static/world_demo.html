A small model that remembers what it sees. (Prototype Demo)

YOLOX detects, SigLIP2 encodes identity, ManifoldFlow stores persistent object basins, and Gemma reads retrieved state through continuous latent tokens.

⚠️ Research prototype, not a validated perception system. The scripted scenarios below (car/box, cup/ball) are pre-recorded and always render correctly — they demonstrate the persistent state mechanism, not live detection. Live camera mode is a different story: the underlying detector was trained on a narrow, mostly-synthetic dataset and frequently misses or misclassifies real objects, including the trained toy car and box themselves. Treat every live-camera answer as a demo of the persistence mechanism working (or not) on top of imperfect, sometimes-wrong perception — not as evidence of general-purpose object recognition. Improved generalization is a future research direction.

Frequently asked questions

How do I use the controls?

The dropdown picks between the two pre-recorded scenarios. Previous Frame/Next Frame step through one frame at a time; Play advances automatically. Try live camera switches to your own webcam with real-time (and much less reliable — see below) detection. End & erase closes the session and deletes everything it remembers about it.

The Ask Gemma about the world box takes any question — about what's currently on screen, or about something you told it earlier in the same session — and answers using whichever mechanism actually has evidence, shown by the colored badge on the reply (see the legend above the chat).

How are sessions isolated?

Every time this page loads, your browser requests a brand-new, cryptographically random session ID from the server — nothing is saved between visits, and no two tabs or visitors ever share one. All persistent state for a session (basin memory and anything you've told Gemma) lives only under that ID on the server, walled off from every other session.

A session auto-expires after ten minutes of inactivity, and closing the tab or clicking End & erase deletes it immediately. Nothing here is meant to persist beyond a single sitting.

What is this demo actually showing?

Most small language models can only answer from what's in their current context — ask about something no longer visible or no longer in the conversation, and they either say they don't know or make something up. This demo pairs a small model (Gemma 3, 270M parameters) with ManifoldFlow, which stores what it has seen or been told as a persistent state outside the model's own context, and injects the relevant piece back in as a vector when it's needed — not by replaying old text into the prompt.

That's what the "visible" vs. "occluded" state actually demonstrates: ask where the car is while it's hidden behind the box, and the answer is grounded in what the system remembers happened, not in anything the current frame could show.

Is this a world model?

Not yet. What's here today is the underpinnings: a persistent state that can accept multimodal writes and be read back by a model as retrieved evidence — the mechanism this demo exists to show off, not a full world model built on top of it.

The next research directions are (1) a model natively trained on that multimodal persistent state, rather than a frozen model reading it via retrieval, and (2) a predictive system in the style of JEPA that forecasts forward using the latents already stored in ManifoldFlow, instead of only answering questions about the past. Temporal data — how state evolves and is predicted over time — is only partially solved today and needs further research.

Why YOLOX, SigLIP2, and Gemma-3-270M?

These are used off-the-shelf to rapidly prove that ManifoldFlow's persistent state can be used by a language model. A future research direction will use our own custom architectures.

What are the two pre-recorded scenarios?

Object permanence (car/box): a toy car drives behind a box, is fully hidden for several frames, then reappears on the other side. Ask "Where is the car?" while it's hidden and a system with no persistent state would have nothing to go on.

Persistent relationships (cup/ball): a ball starts under a cup, then all three cups get shuffled around the table. Ask "Which cup contains the ball?" after the shuffle — the ball is never visible again once covered, so the only way to answer correctly is to have tracked the cup, not the ball.

Both are real rendered frames from the synthetic training corpus (not hand-drawn placeholders), and always play back identically — they exist to show the mechanism working under controlled conditions, not to prove general-purpose perception.

What are the limitations?

This is a research prototype, not a production system. The object detector (live camera mode only) was trained on a narrow, mostly-synthetic dataset and frequently misses or misclassifies real objects, including its own trained classes. Conversational recall was trained on a specific style of fact-tracking question and doesn't yet generalize to arbitrary phrasing or topics — and even when it retrieves the right evidence, Gemma can still misstate or misinterpret it; this needs more training, not just more prompting. Check the Retrieved evidence panel under the chat to see what was actually pulled back before trusting what Gemma did with it. General chat runs through the same small, fine-tuned model, so it can still get ordinary trivia wrong. None of this is hidden — the badges and the banner above are there so you can tell which answers are backed by evidence and which aren't.

How does this fit together?

Two write paths feed one persistent state, which a frozen Gemma reads back from — not by replaying old text, but by taking the retrieved evidence in as latent tokens. ManifoldFlow operates continuously at 5 Hz, tracking state whether or not anyone is asking about it; Gemma is event-driven, only reading that state back in response to a user query.

Before you start the live camera

The object detector behind live mode was trained on a narrow, mostly-synthetic dataset (red/blue/yellow cups and a white ball; a toy car and cardboard box). It is not reliable on real objects, including those exact trained classes — expect missed detections and misclassifications. Live mode exists to show the persistence mechanism working on top of imperfect perception, not to demonstrate general object recognition. For a clean, always-correct demonstration, use the scripted scenarios (Previous/Next/Play) instead.
